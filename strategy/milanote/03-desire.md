# 03 · DESIRE

## Purpose

### stop
prospect thinks: **"fuck, they have taste."**

this is where we resist the instinct to justify ourselves. no tips. no service pitch.
sometimes barely any explanation.

we are establishing one thing: *these people see things I don't see.*
without ever saying "we see things differently."

### the rule
if the caption needs three paragraphs to make the post interesting, **the visual hasn't
done its job**. DESIRE is the least explanatory content we make. often the caption is
four words.

### the question before posting
**can this stop someone before they even know what the post is about?**

### on curation
not every image has to be ours at the start. curated, properly credited, builds the eye
while the original library grows. the ratio should move: 60/40 curated at launch,
20/80 by month six. anything curated gets credit in the first line of the caption, never
buried in a comment.

## Sub-pillars

### visual desire
pure stop-power. one image. no argument.
e.g. a red heel filled with cherries.

### category collisions
makes familiar products feel unfamiliar. the engine of the whole account.
e.g. a handbag art-directed like grocery packaging.

### ordinary / extraordinary
elevates the everyday. a receipt photographed like a luxury campaign.
this one is the cheapest to produce and the most on-brand.

### taste archive
shows what undressed notices. carousels of six references around one idea.
e.g. six brilliant uses of food in fashion imagery.

### creative experiments
demonstrates imagination without a client.
e.g. *what if toothpaste was shot like perfume?*

## The recurring one

### we want to eat this image
a running series, not literally about food.

one image that feels tactile enough to be edible. minimal commentary — often just the
series name. it becomes our own cultural shorthand: after twenty of them, people start
sending us images and saying "this is very *we want to eat this image*."

cadence: roughly every second week. always mode A (full bleed, barely branded).

## Post ideas — ready to make

### 01 · wrong category
image: a shoe filled with raspberries.
> wrong category. right feeling.

that's the whole post.

### 02 · groceries as luxury
a fruit still life photographed like jewellery — hard light, velvet, jeweller's tray.
> make groceries behave like luxury goods.

### 03 · the context changed
a very ordinary product in an absurdly beautiful environment.
> the product didn't change. the context did.

the first DESIRE post that quietly connects to strategy without becoming educational.

### 04 · taste doesn't know categories
carousel of five collisions:
tomato × prada · water × jewellery · butter × fashion · detergent × beauty · a bank card × nightlife
final slide:
> taste doesn't know categories.

### 05 · the receipt
a crumpled receipt, shot on seamless with campaign lighting.
> everything is a product shot if you light it like one.

### 06 · displaced
one object doing another object's job. a sneaker plated with a knife and fork.
> everything is appetising in the right frame.

### 07 · the frame that hides
a face covered by the thing being sold — magazine, mug, packaging.
> you don't need the face. you need the gesture.

### 08 · one colour, six objects
six unrelated objects, all the same red. no caption beyond:
> red is a category now.

### 09 · what if
a creative experiment. toothpaste shot as perfume — glass, shadow, a single drop.
> same rules. different aisle.

### 10 · the taste archive
six references around one principle, credited.
> six times somebody put food where it didn't belong.

## Reference slots

### where the imagery comes from
board `07 visual world` holds the reference library, grouped by principle. DESIRE posts
pull from there. never pull from a general moodboard — pull from a principle, so the post
has an argument even when the caption doesn't.

### shot-list standing order
when there is a production day for a client, always steal 30 minutes at the end for
undressed's own DESIRE library. one collision, one ordinary object, one texture.
that is how the curated ratio drops without a dedicated shoot budget.
