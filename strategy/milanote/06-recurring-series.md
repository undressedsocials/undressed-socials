# 06 · Recurring Series

## How to use this board

### a series is a promise
each of these has a **fixed structure**. same slide count, same beats, same title lozenge,
same caption pattern, every time. that is what makes them recognisable at thumbnail size
and what makes them worth following.

the visual mode varies. the structure never does.

### the numbering
every series post carries a number — `BORROWED CODES 07`. it signals a body of work,
makes them archivable in a highlight, and quietly implies you've been doing this a while.

## BORROWED CODES

### spec
**layer:** THINKING
**question:** what can CATEGORY A steal from WORLD B?
**cadence:** every 2 weeks — the highest-frequency series
**format:** 6 slides
**visual mode:** B (type + imagery), title lozenge in blood · sun

**structure:**
1. the question, set large. `what could a pharmacy steal from fashion?`
2. what the category currently does (one line, unflattering, accurate)
3. what the other world actually does — 4–5 codes, named
4. the translation: what that becomes in this category
5. **what it looks like on social** — the slide that separates us from commentary
6. the turn: one line that makes it apply to the reader's own brand

**caption pattern:** two sentences of context, then the question thrown back:
*what world is your category refusing to look at?*

### the standing rule
we don't borrow the aesthetic. we borrow the **behaviour**.
copying fashion's look is a mood board. stealing fashion's seasonal drop logic is a strategy.

### the queue
- **01** — what could a pharmacy brand steal from fashion? *(casting, scarcity, art direction, drops, seasonal narrative)*
- **02** — what could a bank learn from hospitality? *(arrival, welcome, recognition, concierge, membership — stop treating onboarding as paperwork)*
- **03** — what could austrian tourism steal from music festivals? *(line-ups, anticipation, tiers, aftermovies, the year-round calendar)*
- **04** — what can food steal from fashion? *(seasonal chapters instead of six months of the same hero shot)*
- **05** — what can skincare steal from food? *(texture, ingredient desire, sensory photography, ritual, abundance)*
- **06** — what can a gym steal from nightlife? *(the door, the room, the hour, who's there)*
- **07** — what can a law firm steal from publishing? *(a point of view, an editorial calendar, named authors)*
- **08** — what can a bakery steal from streetwear? *(the drop, the queue, the sell-out, the collab)*
- **09** — what can a furniture brand steal from tennis? *(seasons, whites, clubs, the ritual of a saturday)*
- **10** — what can a b2b software brand steal from magazines? *(a masthead, an issue, a cover story)*

## UNDRESSED

### spec
**layer:** THINKING
**question:** why does this actually work?
**cadence:** every 2 weeks
**format:** 5–8 slides
**visual mode:** B, with the wordmark stamp

**structure:**
1. the headline — a claim, never a topic
2. **what we see** — the evidence. specific, named, current.
3. **why it's happening** — the shift underneath. this is the slide people screenshot.
4. **what it means for brands** — the implication, stated as an instruction
5. the line that lands. optionally: *and if you're wondering what that means for you — that's what we're for.*

**caption pattern:** one paragraph that adds something the carousel didn't say. never a summary.

### the ban
no "here are three things nike does well." that is linkedin.
UNDRESSED strips something down. it does not hand out compliments.

### the queue
- why some brands feel expensive before you see the price
- why nobody wants your perfectly designed feed
- this is not a packaging trend. it's a positioning shift.
- why every new café suddenly looks like a fashion brand
- your rebrand isn't the problem. your world is.
- why everything suddenly looks edible
- why brands are becoming less polished — on purpose
- attention is rented. recognition is owned.
- you probably don't need more content
- why "consistency" is quietly making your brand invisible

## IF IT WERE OURS

### spec
**layer:** MAKING
**question:** what would we actually do with this?
**cadence:** monthly — the highest-value, highest-effort series
**format:** 7 slides
**visual mode:** C (intervention: collage, objects, references, scribbles)

**structure:**
1. `if this [category] were ours` — set large
2. the product, stated flatly. one word if possible.
3. **what we wouldn't build around** — the category's clichés, listed and dismissed
4. **what we would build around** — the world. 6 fragments, no sentences.
5. the casting / the look — 2–3 references
6. **the social executions** — 4–5 named formats, numbered
7. one line that gives it away and invites the transposition

**caption pattern:** name the category, not the brand. then:
*nobody asked us. we did it anyway.*

### the naming rule
use categories and fictional brands, not real named clients or competitors' clients.
"if this water brand were ours" — never "if [real brand] were ours." it keeps the format
generous rather than a public critique, and it lets any brand owner in that category see
themselves in it.

### the queue
water · a bank · a boutique hotel · insurance · a pharmacy · a gym · a bakery ·
an austrian ski resort · a dating app · a furniture brand · a supermarket own-label ·
a dentist

**the insurance one is the flagship:**
> if insurance were ours, we'd stop trying to make insurance interesting.
then build a completely different world around it.

## ONE PRODUCT, THREE WORLDS

### spec
**layer:** MAKING
**question:** how much does context do?
**cadence:** every 6 weeks
**format:** carousel of 4, or a 20-second reel
**visual mode:** A for the three world panels, B for the closing card

**structure:**
1. the product. one object, flat, unstyled, boring on purpose.
2. **world 01** — 5 fragments + one reference image
3. **world 02** — same
4. **world 03** — same
5. closing card: `same product. completely different desire.`

**caption pattern:** one line. *the product never changed.*

### why this one matters most
it is the closest thing to watching the undress happen live. it demonstrates the
methodology in nine seconds, which no case study can do.

### the queue
sparkling water · a white t-shirt · a mortgage · a hand cream · a folding chair ·
a protein bar · a hotel key card · a savings account

## FROM INSIGHT TO EXECUTION

### spec
**layer:** MAKING — but it starts in THINKING
**question:** so what would we actually make?
**cadence:** monthly
**format:** 4–6 slides
**visual mode:** B into A — the argument in type, the payoff in imagery

**structure:**
1. the insight, in one line (usually lifted from an earlier UNDRESSED post)
2. **why** — one sentence
3. **so what** — one sentence
4. `if we were building [X] tomorrow:` — three actual shots, described precisely enough to brief
5. optional: the one creative rule that holds it together

**caption pattern:** link back to the THINKING post it came from. *we said this three weeks ago. here's what we'd do about it.*

### the mechanic
this series is how the harvest rule on board `02` becomes visible. it turns the account
into a body of work with continuity instead of a stream of separate observations —
and it is the cheapest MAKING content to produce, because half the thinking already exists.
